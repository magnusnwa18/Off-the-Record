# Chapter Fourteen — Exposure

*Cal*

The story broke on a Wednesday, which was wrong, which was the first sign that it had slipped everyone's control — Cal had braced for a Thursday, had braced for *managed*, had braced for Sasha's smooth machinery rolling the reveal out on its schedule. Instead his phone went at 7:40 in the morning, in the changing room before training, and it was Knox, and Knox never called, Knox sent eleven voice notes, and the call meant something had gone wrong.

"Cal. Bro. It's out. Someone leaked it. There's a piece — it's got a session photo, it's got the studio, it names you. *Callum Vance.* It's everywhere, man, it's —"

The room went very narrow and very clear.

It was a smaller outlet than Sasha would have chosen — a music blog with a chip on its shoulder, the kind that broke things to spite the people who tried to manage things — and the piece was headlined *EXCLUSIVE: The Secret Behind Knox's New Sound Is a Pro Rugby Prospect,* and it had a photograph, blurry, taken through the high window of the unmarked studio from the building opposite, of Cal at the desk. It had his name. It had the academy. It had a paragraph of breathless speculation about "the double life of the fly-half who could be capped within two years" and another about "questions this raises about his commitment to the sport." It had everything Cal had spent five years burying, dug up and laid out in the worst possible order, and it had been up since 6 a.m., and by 7:40 it had been screenshotted into every group chat in two industries.

Including, Cal knew with a cold certainty that arrived before any other thought, his father's.

He didn't finish the call with Knox. He didn't go to training. He stood in the changing room with the phone in his hand and the lads filtering in around him, some of them already looking at him sideways, already having seen, and he felt the two worlds he'd kept in two boxes for his whole life slam together so hard that the man standing between them simply stopped being able to move.

Tom found him. Tom, who'd known Cal disappeared after dark for two years and never pushed, took one look at his face and steered him out of the room with one enormous arm, into the corridor, away from the eyes.

"I didn't know," Tom said. "The music. Cal, I swear, I didn't — why didn't you *tell* me, man, I'd have —"

"Where's my dad." Cal's voice came out wrong. "Have you seen — is he —"

As if summoned, the phone rang. *Dad.* The screen said *Dad* and Cal looked at it ringing in his hand and could not make himself answer it, because answering it was the moment, the moment he'd organised his entire life to avoid, the moment his father found out that the son he'd saved from being wasted had been doing the wasteful thing in the dark for five years, that the thing that killed Jordan was alive in Cal too, that he'd buried it on purpose so his father would never have to see — and now his father was seeing it on a phone, in a headline, with a blurry photo, the betrayal version, the exact worst version Ella had warned him about, the one where he didn't get to say it himself.

It rang out. Started again immediately. *Dad.*

"Answer it," Tom said quietly. "Whatever it is, mate. Answer it. It's worse if you don't."

Cal answered it.

"Dad."

Silence on the line. A long silence, his father's silence, the invested cold one, except Cal could hear breathing in it, and the breathing wasn't cold, the breathing was something Cal had only heard from his father once before in his life, on a wet night when Cal was sixteen and a phone had rung in a different way.

"Is it true," his father said. Not cold. *Wrecked.* "The — the music. The thing in the — is it you. In the photo. Is it true."

"Dad, I can explain, I was going to —"

"*Is it true, Callum.*"

"Yes," Cal said. "It's true. It's me. I make the — I've been making it for years. I'm sorry. I'm so sorry, I never wanted you to find out like —"

And his father made a sound that Cal would hear for the rest of his life, a sound with no words in it, the sound of a man who'd buried one son for loving this exact thing and had just learned his other son had loved it in secret the whole time, right under him, and the sound contained grief and betrayal and fear and something underneath all of those that Cal was too far gone to hear, and then his father said, in a voice gone flat and final, "Don't come to the house," and ended the call.

*Don't come to the house.*

The thing Cal had spent five years and one buried brother's voice avoiding had happened in a single Wednesday morning, on someone else's terms, in the worst order, and it had cost him exactly what he'd always known it would cost him.

---

He didn't go back to training. He didn't go to the studio — the studio was burned now, photographed, a location, the one room where he'd been whole turned into evidence. He walked. He walked through the grey city for hours, hood up, phone off, past the warehouse where they'd shot the single and the chip shop in Mayfield and the streets that had been theirs, and somewhere in the walking the cold narrow clarity hardened into something he recognised from when he was sixteen, the thing he'd built the ghost out of: *if you're already gone, you can't be left.* The ghost was safe. The ghost had done the leaving first.

So when he finally turned his phone on, at dark, and it lit up with everything — the missed calls, the texts from the lads, from Knox, from Sasha (*we can fix this, call me, we control the next part of the narrative*), from Imogen Clarke-Bayo of all people, and from Ella, eleven messages from Ella, *are you okay, please answer, Cal it wasn't me, please believe me it wasn't me, where are you, please, I think I know how to fix it, please call me* — Cal looked at Ella's name eleven times on the screen and did the ghost thing.

He'd told her about the offer that morning, days ago — she'd shown him the folded thing, told him the whole shape of the catch, *everything I want is downstream of your reveal, I can't win clean.* And now the reveal had happened, and the supernova was coming, and the offer was about to become as big as it could be, and the timing of it sat in Cal's wrecked mind and rearranged itself into the worst possible story, because that was what wrecked minds did, they were just bad producers, chopping the truth into the cruelest order.

*She said she couldn't win clean unless the reveal happened. The reveal happened. From a small blog that breaks things to spite the people managing them. The kind of blog you'd leak to if you wanted the reveal to happen but didn't want it traced back to a fixer. And now her offer's about to go supernova.*

He knew, even as he thought it, that it was the wrecked-mind version. He knew Ella. He knew her better than he'd known anyone. He knew she'd kept his secret when Sasha offered to make her famous, knew she'd lied to Imogen Clarke-Bayo's face about who he was, knew she'd texted him *it wasn't me* before he'd even accused her of anything, which is what innocent people do and what guilty people are also smart enough to do, said the ghost, the cold narrow ghost, and Cal was too far gone, too sixteen, too *don't come to the house* to tell the difference between the truth and the thing that would let him do the leaving first.

He texted her back. One line. The cruelest order he could chop it into.

*Everything you wanted is downstream of this. Congratulations. You win clean after all.*

Then he turned the phone off again, before she could reply, before she could be innocent at him, before she could see him, because being seen was the whole danger and he was done with the danger, and he walked back out into the city that wasn't theirs anymore, the ghost again, safe again, already gone, and he told himself it was better this way, the way he'd told himself everything for five years, and he almost believed it, which had always been the problem.

The catch had everything now. It had taken the morning too.
