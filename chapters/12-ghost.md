# Chapter Twelve — Ghost

*Cal*

He found out about Sasha's visit the way he found out everything that mattered, which was sideways and too late — Knox mentioned it, careless, in the middle of a session, *yeah Sash said she ran the reveal thing past you and Ella, said you're thinking about it,* and Cal went very still, the counting stillness, and looked across the studio at Ella, who had gone the specific colour of someone caught holding something.

"You're thinking about it," Cal repeated, to her, when Knox had gone out for food.

"I'm not thinking about anything. She came to me too. Three days ago. She did the whole — the story's coming whether you manage it or not, someone always talks, be the hero or be the collateral routine. And she —" Ella stopped. "She looked at the toothbrush, Cal. She knows about us. Or she's guessing, and she's good enough that the guess is as dangerous as knowing. She's going to use one of us to get the reveal and I didn't know how to tell you so I —"

"You held it." His voice was level. Dangerous-level. "Three days. You sat on the single most important thing that's happened to me since Jordan died and you *held it.* For three days. Why."

"Because I wanted to give you one more good morning before the world came in!" It came out of her in a rush, the real thing, and he could see she'd been carrying it alone and it had cost her, and some part of him registered that and most of him was too far gone into the cold narrow place to care. "Because you said the catch could have everything else but not the morning, and I wanted to give you a clean one, I wanted —"

"You wanted to carry it for me."

She stopped.

"That's what this is," Cal said. "You did the exact thing. The thing you told me you'd never do, the thing you swore at me about at Knox's playback — *I will not be a thing you protect, I need a partner not a bodyguard* — you stood there and you made that whole speech and then the second it's a weight *you* could carry alone to spare *me*, you did it without even —" He was on his feet. He didn't remember standing. "Three days, Ella. Sasha's been building a story about me for three days with my — with the person who's supposed to be on my side sitting on it to protect my *feelings.* Do you know what that is? That's exactly what my dad does. He decides what I can handle and he manages me. He's been doing it for sixteen years. I cannot be with someone who *manages* me."

It was unfair and he knew it was unfair even as it left him, knew he was taking the fear of Sasha and the fear of his father and the fear of the reveal and pointing all of it at the one safe target, the one person who'd stay and take it, which was its own kind of cruelty, doing the thing to her that he was accusing her of — deciding she could handle being the place he put the fear. But he was a furnace with the lid off and the wanting had dragged all the locked rooms open and he didn't have the control yet, the ten thousand reps weren't enough, and it came out as fire.

"That's not fair," Ella said, quiet, and he watched her go somewhere he'd never seen her go, somewhere cold and shut, the strong-one place, the place she went when she decided to stop letting someone in. "I held it for three days because I'm an idiot who loves you and I've spent my whole life believing the way you love people is you carry their weight so they don't have to feel it. And you're right, that's exactly what I swore I wouldn't do, and I did it, and I'm sorry. But you're not actually angry that I carried it. You're angry that the reveal's coming and you can't control it, and I'm the only person in the room you're allowed to be angry at, because you can't be angry at Sasha and you can't be angry at your dad and you can't be angry at Jordan for dying, so you're putting all of it on me. And I'll take a lot, Cal. I've taken a lot my whole life, I'm *built* for taking it. But I won't be the place you put the thing you're too scared to point where it belongs. I did that for my whole family. I'm not doing it for you."

The studio was completely silent. The city tumbled on below, indifferent, full of people each carrying their one unbearable thing, and here were two of them, doing the worst thing two people who love each other can do, which is be right about each other at the same time.

"So what do you want me to do," Cal said. Lower now. The fire banking, not out.

"I want you to tell your dad."

"*What?*"

"Not for me. Not for the project. For *you.*" She stepped toward him, and the cold-shut place cracked, and underneath it was just her, just Ella, exhausted and frightened and so far past managing him that she was finally just *telling* him. "Sasha's right about one thing and only one thing — the reveal is coming. Someone always talks. It might be a leaked session photo, it might be a tagged location, it might be *her*, but the day is coming when your dad finds out V is you whether you choose it or not. And there are two versions of that day, Cal. In one version, he finds out from a culture site, from a headline, from someone at the club showing him on their phone — *here's your son, the secret producer, here's the thing he's been hiding for five years* — and it's a betrayal, it's the worst possible version, it confirms every fear he's got, that the music is a shameful secret thing that takes his sons away in the dark. And in the other version —" her voice broke and she let it "— in the other version *you* tell him. Before the story does. You sit him down and you play him the buried track and you tell him whose voice is in it, and you say: *Dad, the thing that made the pass different on Saturday is the same thing that was in Jordan, it's not a waste, it's not the thing that killed him, it's the best thing in both of us, and I've been hiding it because I thought it would make me lose you, and I'm done hiding it.* And maybe he can't hear it. Maybe it goes badly. I told you, I'd never promise it goes well. But it'll be *yours.* You'll have said the true thing out loud before someone else said it for you. And that's the only version where you're not a ghost anymore."

Cal stood in the studio where he kept his brother buried in the speakers, and looked at the one person who'd ever stood in all his rooms at once, and felt the fire go all the way out, leaving the cold thing underneath it, the thing that had been there since he was sixteen and didn't reply to a voice note for two days.

"I can't lose him too," Cal said. It came out very small, the smallest he'd ever been in front of anyone. "I lost Jordan. I can't — if I tell him and he can't hear it, if he looks at me the way he — I can't lose the last one too, Ella. I'm not strong enough. I know you think I am, everyone thinks I am, the still one, the magic, but I'm *not*, that's the whole — I built the ghost because the ghost can't lose anyone. The ghost's already gone. You can't bury a man who's already buried himself."

And there it was, the actual centre, deeper than Jordan, deeper than the track — the thing the whole architecture of his life had been built to avoid: that Cal Vance had made himself into a ghost on purpose, had buried his own name alongside his brother's voice, because a ghost was safe, a ghost couldn't be left, a ghost had already done the leaving first. The detachment wasn't cool. It was a held breath he'd been holding for five years so the grief couldn't get back in.

Ella crossed the last of the distance and she didn't make a speech and she didn't manage him and she didn't carry it for him. She just put her arms around him, finally, the way she hadn't the night he told her about Jordan, and she held on, and she said into his chest, "You're not a ghost. I can see you. I've been able to see you since the loudest room in Fallowfield. You came over anyway, remember? Ghosts don't do that. Ghosts stay in the corner. You crossed the room." She pulled back to look at him. "You keep saying you're not strong enough. Cal. You've been carrying a dead brother and a terrified father and a secret self for five years *alone*, with no one even knowing the weight was there. That's not weakness. That's the strongest thing I've ever seen anyone do. But you don't have to do it alone anymore. That's the whole — that's the only thing I've been trying to say. Put it down. In the middle. Where I can see it. We carry it together or we don't carry it at all, but you don't get to be a ghost anymore, because I've seen you, and you can't un-see-able yourself to me now. It's too late. I already know your name."

And Cal Vance, the still one, the magic, the ghost, put his head down on the shoulder of the only person who'd ever seen all of him at once, in the unmarked studio above the vintage shop with his brother's voice climbing and falling in the dark, and for the second time in his life he let someone watch him come apart, and this time she didn't just stay beside him with her hand on the wood.

This time she held on.
