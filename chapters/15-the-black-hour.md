# Chapter Fifteen — The Black Hour

*Ella*

She read his text in the launderette-warm flat with the dryers tumbling beneath her, and she read it four times, and each time it took something else out of her.

*Everything you wanted is downstream of this. Congratulations. You win clean after all.*

He thought she'd done it. After everything — after Jordan, after the buried track, after *you can't un-see-able yourself to me now, I already know your name* — he thought she'd leaked him. He thought she'd taken the one thing he'd ever trusted anyone with and sold it for the offer. He'd done the cold thing, the calculation, run the odds on her loyalty against her ambition and decided the ambition won, because that was how he was built, he read odds, and the odds said the girl whose whole career was downstream of the reveal had made the reveal happen.

The cruelty of it was surgical. *Congratulations.* He knew exactly where she was softest — the fear that she was, underneath the loyalty, just ambitious; the thing her mother had named, *a hobby you're calling a career*, the worry she'd never said out loud to anyone that maybe she really would step on anyone to prove the thing she was could be a life. He'd found that exact place and put the knife in it, and he'd done it on purpose, because hurting her was the fastest way to make her leave first, and she understood — she could read him even now, even through the wreckage — that he was doing the ghost thing, the *if you're already gone you can't be left* thing, that he was burning it down so the loss would be his choice instead of his fear.

She understood it completely. And understanding it didn't help at all, because the thing about being understood-out-of-cruelty is that it still cuts, the knife doesn't care that you can see the hand.

Priya came home to find her on the floor in the warm, not crying, which was worse, the strong-one not-crying, the not-crying she'd learned from her mother at the kitchen table at midnight.

"I saw the piece," Priya said carefully, lowering herself down. "Tom's a wreck, the whole team's — is Cal okay? Have you —"

"He thinks I did it." Ella's voice came out flat. "He thinks I leaked him. For the offer."

"*What?* That's insane, you'd never, you kept it when Sasha —"

"I know."

"Did you tell him —"

"I told him eleven times before he even accused me. I said *it wasn't me* before he said anything. And he texted back —" she showed Priya the phone, and watched Priya's face do the thing, the protective fury, the *I'll kill him* thing, and held up a hand. "Don't. He's not — he's doing the thing he does. He lost his dad this morning, Pri. His dad told him don't come to the house. And his whole life is built on this idea that if he leaves first he can't be left, so he's burning me down before I can — it's not about me. I know it's not about me. He's just —" her voice finally cracked, one clean fracture "— he's just pointing it at the only person who'll stay and take it. Which is the exact thing I told him I wouldn't be. And the exact thing I keep being anyway."

"That's not your fault."

"It doesn't have to be my fault to be the thing that's happening." Ella wiped her face once, hard, and put the strong-one back on, because there was no one underneath her to catch the pieces, there never was, that was the whole architecture of her life. "And here's the part that's actually funny. He thinks I won. He thinks everything I wanted is downstream of this, he thinks I'm sitting here celebrating my supernova. And I was about to —" she laughed, and it was a terrible laugh "— Pri, I had a plan. I texted Sasha *let's get coffee.* I was going to take the story away from her. I'd worked out how. I was going to fix it so the reveal happened on *Cal's* terms or didn't happen at all, so that I could have the offer *and* protect him, so that nobody got to use either of us — and I was three days from doing it, and some blog with a chip on its shoulder beat me to it, and now Cal thinks I'm the blog, and I can't even — there's no version where I get to tell him the truth, because the truth is *I was about to fix it* and that sounds exactly like a lie a guilty person tells."

Priya was quiet for a long moment, the dryers tumbling. "So what do you do."

And there it was — the moment Ella had been walking toward since the train outside Stockport, the choice that wasn't really about Cal at all, the one Imogen had named: *most people never find out who they really are because they never have to choose between what they want and who they want to be.*

Because here was the brutal arithmetic, laid bare now that the reveal had happened the worst way: Cal had cut her loose. He thought she'd betrayed him. The smart thing — the strong-one thing, the protect-yourself thing, the thing her mother would counsel and her ambition would thank her for — was to take the offer. Sign it. Let the supernova carry her to London and the seat at Imogen's studio and the answer to six o'clock. Cal didn't want her anymore; he'd made that surgically clear. Her loyalty had already cost her once by nearly shrinking the offer; why should it cost her again now, when the person she'd been loyal to had just knifed her on his way out the door? Take the win. Be free. Stop carrying.

She could hear the argument perfectly. It was a good argument. It was, in its way, the same argument her whole family had been making her whole life: *be safe, protect yourself, the world will not protect you, so build something no one can take.*

And she found, sitting on the floor in the warm with the worst day of her life tumbling around her, that she didn't want it. Not the safe version. Not the win that cost Cal his terms. Not the seat that she got by riding the supernova of his betrayal. Because the thing she'd realised on the train outside Stockport was still true even now that he'd hurt her — *maybe the truest thing I've ever found is a weight I'm glad to pick up: fighting for someone else's right to be seen instead of just protecting my own.* That was who she wanted to be. And who she wanted to be didn't get switched off because the person she was fighting for had stopped, in his terror, being able to see her.

"I'm going to do the thing I was going to do anyway," Ella said slowly. "I'm going to take the story away from the people telling it. Not for him — he's made it pretty clear I'm not doing anything for him. For *me.* Because I refuse to let the version where I'm the villain be the version that's true, and the only way to make a different version true is to go and build it, the way I build everything." She got up off the floor. She felt the strong-one settle onto her, except it was different this time, it wasn't the exhausted mask, it was something she was choosing, the way Cal had chosen to play the second half loose. "And maybe it fixes things with Cal and maybe it doesn't. Maybe he never believes it wasn't me. Maybe his dad never speaks to him again and he spends the rest of his life being a ghost and I just — watch from London. But I'm not going to let the last true thing I did be carrying his weight in secret to protect his feelings and getting knifed for it. The last true thing I do is going to be *loud.*"

"That's terrifying," Priya said. "That's the most you thing you've ever said. What are you actually going to do?"

Ella picked up her phone. Sasha had replied to *let's get coffee* an hour ago, before the leak, with a smiling *Always! When?* — not knowing the blog was about to take the reveal out of her hands too, not knowing that her carefully seeded story had been stolen by a spiteful outlet and that her own control was as gone as everyone else's.

"Sasha built a story where me and Cal had to be enemies so one of us would crack and the reveal would come," Ella said. "She's a producer, basically. She chops the truth into the order that serves her. And the thing about a producer —" Ella's eyes did the thing, the reading thing, except now she was reading three moves ahead, the way Cal read a defensive line "— is that anyone can do it. You just need command of the elements. And I've got an element nobody else has. I know who V actually is. I know about Jordan. I know what the buried track is and whose voice is in it. I know the *real* story, the one that's a thousand times bigger and truer than 'rugby boy has secret hobby.' And the blog only got the shallow version, the betrayal version. So there's still a deeper version to tell." She looked at Priya. "The question is whether I've got any right to tell it. Because it's not my secret. It's his. And telling someone's deepest truth to save them is just betraying them in a nicer font, unless —"

She stopped. The plan finished assembling itself in her head, and it was good, and it was terrifying, and it required the one thing she wasn't sure she could get, which was Cal — the real Cal, the one under the ghost — to choose, himself, to be seen.

"Unless he tells it," Ella finished quietly. "Unless I build him a stage and a moment and a world so safe and so true that the ghost decides, on his own, to step out of the corner and cross the room. Because that's the only version where it's not me unmasking him. It's him taking off the mask." She looked at her own hands. "I can't make him do it. I can only build the room. The way I built the room at the party that I didn't know I was building. The way I always build the room."

"And if he won't step out?"

"Then I built a room for nobody," Ella said. "And I'll have found out who I am anyway. Which Imogen says is the whole point, and which I'm starting to think is the most expensive thing there is."

She sat back down. She opened the laptop. The blue light came up onto her face the way it had the night Cal told her about Jordan, and outside the rain came down on the grey glittering city, and Eleonora Savage-Oluwa, who had been the strong one her whole life and never once for herself, began to build the most important world of her career for an audience of one terrified ghost who'd just told her she'd won, and might never know that the only thing she was actually trying to win was him.
